## Mechanics (26.3 source, verified in the game)

- A hopper pulls from any container ENTITY whose box overlaps the 1x1x1 cube above it (a random one each try). A hopper minecart (0.98 wide) straddling a cell edge feeds 2 hoppers; off-rail at a corner, 4.
- A hopper minecart sucks the block container at containing(x, y + 1.5, z): its centre must be inside the box's column, one cell under the box. 1 item/gt.
- With no container above, a hopper minecart sucks item entities in the 1x1 column from 0.69 to 2.0 above its own level, i.e. inside the box cell: it swallows the broken box's drop in the very tick it spawns (block events run before entities) unless it is full or disabled.
- Off-rail carts cannot be disabled. They are safe only when every slot still holds items at break time: true for 64- and 16-stack items, false for unstackables (a slot empties within a few gt).
- An activator rail powered in the same redstone update as the breaking piston disables carts on it before they tick (activateMinecart runs in the cart's tick before it sucks).
- Stationary carts keep the yaw they were spawned with. Two carts push each other only if |direction . facing| >= 0.8 within 0.2 of each other, so yaw 45 lets carts sit side by side in x or z.
- Never put a cart centre exactly on a cell edge (z = 0.0): a 90 degree turn maps it to -0.0 and the wrong column (rotations failed until 0.01).
- Loader face budget with the dispenser above the box: dispenser, piston stone, drop exit, comparator read-through and ONE feeding hopper (a hopper under the box would pull). So a hopper loader is capped at 2.5 items/s per box. More needs droppers (5/s per face, dropper under the box facing up), which must be gated: a dropper facing air throws its item. Gate idea: subtract comparator, rear = the box level L, side = a clock (pulses only while L > 0), first item from the hopper. Tried a second hopper with the drop leaving downward (loader-2h.txt): the drop escapes, no good.
- Rail unloader tiles at a 6-block pitch; the off-rail one cannot get `tileable` (the badge runs with unstackables).

## Smallest loader: tried, no gain yet

clk-rt's always-on loader (31b) is near-minimal for its architecture. Tried: comparator reading the box directly (no read-through stone), the redstone block doubling as the drop cell's north wall, 4 dust straight into the piston (29b, loader-small-directread-FAILS.txt). Fails: when the piston's stone fills the box cell, a comparator next to the cell reads THROUGH the stone into the feed hopper behind it, the hopper fills during the swap, reads 15, and the piston stays extended forever. The read-through stone exists for that reason: the comparator then reads the pushed stone itself (0). Feed hopper and comparator sit on opposite faces of the box cell (the piston and the drop exit take the other horizontal pair), so a direct read always sees the feed hopper.

## Mixed badge: analysis (2026-10-20, from the judge and the 26.3 source)

The scenario: rare iron x3 first, then 16 rounds of cobble 64, snowball 16, dirt 37, cobble 20, snowball 5, dirt 64 (3299 items, 6 boxes supplied). Every box but the last needs 27 occupied slots, and the last box must arrive within 6000 gt of the source running dry, so mixed needs a FLUSH too.
- A hopper or dropper puts an item into the first slot that is empty or can merge. A loader's box fills its slots in order, so its empty slots always come after its occupied ones, and each item kind has at most one partial stack. A box with an empty slot accepts any item, so a blocked feed means the box has 27 occupied slots: a jam detector is exactly right.
- Boxes without the rare item end at 15 as the cycle tops up every partial stack. The iron box ends at 26 full stacks + iron 3 = level 14 and jams there.
- Backlog detector: a feeder that drains faster than it is fed (a dropper on a 4 gt clock fed at 2.5/s) holds ~0-1 items, and a jam makes it grow. Its comparator reading 3 or more = jam (2 shovels give 4, 1 shovel gives 2; ~82 cobble). A plain hopper feeder does NOT work: it pulls while it can't push during each box swap, so its resting backlog grows ~2 items per swap (a hopper is full of shovels after 2-3 swaps).
- Flush with restart: the flush timeout must outlast the 3000 gt restart pause and fire within 6000 gt. Plan: an HOURGLASS, a dropper preloaded with ~500 `minecraft:stone` items (not a scenario item, so the judge ignores it), drained by a hopper that is unlocked only while (box level > 0) AND (feed hopper empty). It drains 375 items during a restart pause and finishes ~1000-4000 gt after the input really ends, and then nothing moves (it is empty), so the end-of-run rest check passes. Its comparator falling to 0 fires the piston. Without the box-level condition it would run under the empty spare box at the end of the base run and fail the rest check.

Status 2026-10-20 01:30: ARCHIVED shulker-mixed-loader (58b): clk-rt's flush loader + the jam detector (comparator H on the feeding hopper N -> 4 dust: on at N level >= 4 -> subtract-comparator rising edge -> 4 gt piston pulse). It passes mixed, flush, unstackable and drop-stress; it misses only restart. The plain-hopper resting-backlog worry did not show in the game: the swap is shorter than N's 8 gt cooldown.

Hourglass for restart + flush (open): the lock must be OR(items chest non-empty, dispenser empty). "Box level == 0" works too but needs a raw box reader, and the loader's C is compare+B. Pitfalls found on paper:
- A comparator's front block is strongly powered and powers adjacent DUST, so the hourglass output must stay away from the jam dust and from the always-on dust (1,4,0)/(0,3,0).
- E's front block (3,2,0) is strongly powered while the dispenser holds boxes, so the hourglass hopper must not touch it (wrong polarity). Use a standing torch on top of it, which is lit when the dispenser is empty.
- Two hoppers in series (one lock each) leak: the upstream one fills its 320-item buffer while the downstream one is locked.
Next try: build the hourglass away from the core (x >= 3), with clean routes: chest comparator CH -> repeater -> stone under the hourglass hopper; E -> stone -> standing torch beside the hopper; the hourglass comparator -> torch -> a repeater line to the piston's top (a steady signal after expiry is fine: the piston stays extended at the end).

Hourglass result (loader-mixed-rs.txt, 79b): flush and mixed pass with the hourglass (box 3 at 3903 gt in the flush run). Restart FAILS, and the cause is NOT the hourglass: the jam detector is noisy with shovels. The plain feeding hopper N under the judge's chest already holds 2 shovels before the first box (it pulls while there is no box), 3-4 while a full box waits to break, and 0 during the restart pause. A shovel is worth 3 comparator levels, so N's level crosses any threshold at swaps and after the pause, and a crossing just after a placement breaks the new empty box. Threshold 4 got through the plain runs only because N's 2-shovel equilibrium (level 6) keeps the chain high, so no edges come. Threshold 7 (a composter at 3 on the subtract side of H) breaks box 2 empty right away. Conclusion: a jam detector on a plain hopper's backlog cannot coexist with restart. It needs a feeder that drains faster than it is fed: a dropper firing every 4 gt (5/s) fed by ONE hopper line (2.5/s), whose comparator stays ~0 except in a jam. That fits a fast-loader variant (N 2.5/s + Y 2.5/s = 5/s, Y gated to 0 < L < 15, its level as the jam signal) plus the hourglass.

## Loader past 7.5/s: analysis (2026-10-20)

- A box takes items only from hoppers (2.5/s each) and droppers (one dispense per 4 gt = 5/s: each rising edge schedules a tick 4 gt later, and a second schedule while one is pending is dropped). Dispensers drop items instead of inserting them, and hopper minecarts only pull.
- Faces of the box cell: the dispenser D (placement), the pushed stone and the drop cell (an opposite pair), C's read stone, and two free faces. Two droppers (below + south) => 10/s on one box. dropper75 uses hopper N + dropper Y = 7.5/s.
- Supply: 3 item chests via hoppers = 7.5/s. The judge's initial fill holds the whole budget in a, b, c (81 stacks), so a 4th chest stays empty. More supply needs a hopper minecart under a chest, at a cell edge so it straddles 2 hoppers: 5/s from one chest.
- Retrofitting a second dropper into dropper75 is crowded. A solid block under the south dropper, powered by an observer on the gate loop, also powers Y, and the union of Y's pulses can stay on: no falling edge, so Y stops firing. A hopper under the south dropper pulls from it. A fresh layout is needed.
- Parallel stations: 4 auto-loaders in a pinwheel around one hopper minecart (2x2 hoppers under it, each the feed hopper of one box) works on paper. Each box keeps a free opposite pair for the piston and drop, but each station's always-on logic (E, Q, Q2) crowds the centre.
- Conduction gating is out: a closed shulker box IS a redstone conductor (default isRedstoneConductor = full collision shape, closed box = full block). But a dropper UNDER the box also fires through quasi-connectivity from anything pointing into the box cell, box or air. And a free-running clock fails the rest check.
- The in-game shrink tool keeps badges, not speed. `circuit shulker shrink --min-rate R` (added) also keeps the plain 64-stack items/s >= R. It still does not run the drop stress, so walls it removes must be checked: the final verify reports fragile, and the drop-cell walls go back by hand.
- Measured: dropper75 without its second observer (the one under the stone north of Y, watching the delay-2 copy of G) drops from 7.39 to 4.11 items/s. One observer on the 8 gt comparator clock fires Y only every 8 gt, so each dropper needs two observers in opposite phases, or one observer on a dust carrying (G OR its 4 gt-late copy). Two droppers therefore need either a shared solid block adjacent to both (south of Y = under the south dropper), with both observers outputting into it and no other power into either dropper (else the union of pulses can stay on and the dropper sees no edge), or a second gated clock for the second dropper.
- Cleanest fresh layout found on paper: the dispenser on the SOUTH face (facing north), droppers on top (facing down, fired from above, where space is free) and bottom, C reading the north stone, piston west, drop east. A solid block under the south dispenser may be pulsed: the dispenser firing at an occupied cell fails harmlessly and keeps the box. The bottom dropper's feeders: west + north (Y's south cell is under the dispenser, so no hopper there).

## Fast loader + hourglass flush (loader-d75-flush.txt, 115b, WIP 2026-10-20)

dropper75 plus an east-side hourglass: Tc (500 stone) over Hd, which drains into a sink. Hd is locked by comparator CH on chest b and by a wall torch under it that is lit when D is empty (A's output stone). Comparator HG on Tc feeds a stone; the torch on that stone is lit when Tc is empty. From it, a dust staircase feeds repeater I, which points into the read stone, so c reads 15, a fake full that fires the piston.
- Locking on chest b is safe: the judge tops every source chest back up every tick while the budget lasts, so all chests run dry together. In restart (unstackables) a=27 and b=13 are supplied at t=0 and the pause starts at t=1, so b runs dry ~100 gt before a. The hourglass then drains ~2900 gt < 4000.
- Restart: PASS. Flush: FAIL, "box 3 arrived with 1 of 27". Plain dropper75 fails flush the same way: after box 2, all 13 leftover shovels sit in Y and its feed hoppers (Y full with 9), and N is empty. So box 3 never gets the first item from N. Its level stays 0, the 0<L<15 gate stays shut, and Y never fires.
- Root cause, common to (a), (b) and (c): the dropper gate needs "box present", and the only reader we have is L>0. So the first item must come from a hopper, and leftovers stranded in a dropper chain are lost.
- Ways out, not built yet:
  (1) Force the gate open in an hourglass stage 1, then fake full in stage 2. That needs two expiry times: two hourglasses, or Tc level <= 1 via one dust decay. Force only between the stages, or the clock never rests.
  (2) Box presence by conduction: a closed shulker box is a conductor, so a SIDE dropper next to X is powered through the box only when a box is there. Dust on K pointing into X powers it. But the bottom dropper also sees X's power by QC, and dust on K locks K while it is on.
  (3) The A observer is no placement detector: comparator level changes 15->13 leave the block state alone, so it fires only on empty<->non-empty.
- Mixed + restart: a threshold on N cannot separate the two cases. Shovel noise reaches level 12 (4 shovels during a full wait); a mixed jam can plateau at level 9-11 (5 slots of partial stacks of different kinds). It needs a persistence filter (on > ~64 gt). That is either 8 delay-4 repeaters or a hopper integrator. A dropper feeder has the same swap backlog unless the gate opens on box presence.

- The fix for all three items, found on paper (not built yet). It gives the box its first item from a dropper at each placement:
  - Placement detector: comparator A on D outputs into a DUST. A placement changes D's level (15 -> 13 ...), so the dust's power state changes and an observer on that dust fires. A comparator's own block state doesn't change, so an observer on A itself does not fire.
  - One-shot: a dot (cross-shaped) dust on top of K, in the drop cell, points west into X. Pulse it for 2 gt by strongly powering the drop-cell wall stone (2,1,0). A repeater or observer pointing into the dust would connect it into a line that no longer points into X.
  - The dust strongly powers the closed box (a conductor), so every dropper next to X fires once: the bottom one by QC, side ones by conduction. D also fires, harmlessly; K and N lock for 2 gt.
  - Arrivals also change D's level, so the one-shot fires again then, into a present box; harmless.
  - The one-shot must reach the droppers >= 2 gt after D's dispense.
  - This removes the need for a hopper first item: the 10/s two-dropper loader becomes possible, and leftovers stranded in Y get into the flush box.

## Combo (loader + unloader, `port input.mode` lever): plan

One items chest cannot be both the loader's source (above the feed hopper) and the unloader's sink (fed by the hopper under the box), and the judge fills the item chests in name order with 81 stacks, so a sink named items.b would get 27 stacks in loader mode. Plan: loader sources items.a/b/c into one pool hopper over the feed hopper, unloader sink items.d (gets no budget in loader mode); one comparator reading the box through the read stone feeds both detectors (compare with a redstone block = full; squared + subtract pulse = became empty); the mode lever locks the full-detector repeater (unloader mode) and, through a torch, the unloading hopper and the empty-pulse path (loader mode).

Better plan (one detector chain, one path to the piston): S = (L > 0) AND NOT(full AND loader mode). Build it as in the fast loader: C -> dust A (L) -> Z (subtract, rear A, side = K2's full signal through repeater f) -> W (square) = S. Then a falling-edge pulse V (subtract: side = S direct, rear = S 4 gt late) fires the piston. Loader mode: S falls when the box reaches 15 (pulse, break); when the full box breaks L goes 15 -> 0 but S was already 0 (no pulse). Unloader mode: the mode lever locks f, so S = (L > 0) and falls when the box empties. Plus a torch on the lever's block that locks the unloading hopper H in loader mode. Layout notes for that plan (around clk-rt's always-on loader, west piston): C (0,1,-2) raw -> dust A (0,1,-3); K2 (0,1,-4) facing south + B (1,1,-4); K2 -> (0,1,-5) -> (-1,1,-5) -> f (-1,1,-4) facing south into Z's north side; Z (-1,1,-3) subtract facing east; W (-2,1,-3) facing east -> S dust (-3,1,-3) = V's north side; S -> (-4,1,-3) -> (-5,1,-3) -> (-5,1,-2) -> T2 (-4,1,-2) delay 2 facing west -> V (-3,1,-2) subtract facing west -> dust (-2,1,-2) -> stone (-2,1,-1) -> piston. Pitfalls found on paper: nothing may touch C's sides ((+-1,1,-2)); B must not touch any dust; the mode lever must not touch the S dust (it would hold V's side on), so f's lock needs a repeater chain from the west; the unloading hopper H (0,0,0) is locked in loader mode most cheaply by a torch under it (0,-1,0) on a block the mode lever powers, so the lever wants to sit near H and the f lock near C: the two locks pull the lever to opposite places. Unsolved: one lever position reaching both cheaply.
